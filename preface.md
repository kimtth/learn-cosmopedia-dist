# Preface

Cosmopedia aims to create an encyclopedia that is concise and accessible, rather than reproducing the immense volume and complex writing of traditional general encyclopedias. It brings together knowledge and diverse perspectives in a convenient reference work that readers can consult with ease or read from beginning to end. It also seeks to offer a broad overview of human knowledge and provide a foundation for further applications, including natural language processing and knowledge graphs.

Traditional encyclopedias have had three major limitations.

First, encyclopedias compiled from the perspective of a particular country have tended to reflect that country's interests and viewpoints, limiting the scope and depth of their coverage. Since the nineteenth century, encyclopedia publishing has often been shaped by nationalism, with countries giving priority to their own history, people, geography, and culture.

Second, encyclopedias have often been books to own rather than books to read. Although kept on bookshelves, their weight and the difficulty of finding specific information made them inconvenient for everyday use. Some entries have also been criticized for being too complex and lengthy for general readers.

Third, the longer and more complex entries become, the harder it is to verify and update individual facts. When related information is scattered across multiple entries, it is also difficult to see how those facts are connected. On Wikipedia, an editor's interest in a particular subject can result in coverage that is disproportionate to its historical significance. Although Wikipedia adopts a neutral point of view as an editorial principle, that balance can be undermined when a particular perspective receives too much emphasis during the editing process.

Meanwhile, the publication of general print encyclopedias largely came to an end around the early to mid-2000s. With the growth of the internet, specialized information and detailed facts have become readily accessible through Wikipedia, other online resources, and generative AI. However, these sources tend to focus on detailed information about individual topics, making it difficult for readers to explore unfamiliar fields and gain an overview of them. Some countries continue to publish medium-sized dictionaries that incorporate encyclopedic content. Their continued publication demonstrates that single-volume reference works, including print dictionaries, remain useful for quickly understanding the basic facts about a term or person without having to consult more detailed sources.

## Source Selection

The selected encyclopedias complement one another through their different geographic and cultural perspectives. *Britannica Concise Encyclopedia* provides a global perspective, while *The Columbia Encyclopedia* offers an additional Western perspective. *MyPedia* and the *JoongAng Multimedia Encyclopedia* help fill the gap in Asian perspectives. They cover people, places, and customs from the perspectives of their respective societies and examine certain global topics from an East Asian perspective.

The headwords were drawn from these encyclopedias. Topics selected from yearbooks published in subsequent years were incorporated as new entries or additional paragraphs in existing ones. Verified population statistics and administrative boundary changes were reflected in the relevant entries, and identified errors were corrected based on supporting evidence.

## To the Reader

Cosmopedia brings together a wide range of knowledge and perspectives to help readers develop a broader understanding of the world. We hope it will encourage readers to venture beyond familiar subjects, explore unfamiliar fields, and discover connections between different areas of knowledge.
